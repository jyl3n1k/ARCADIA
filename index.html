Abstract

Robotic and embodied sensing systems can generate large volumes of image and video data that are difficult to interpret as a coherent representation of an environment.

We present Arcadia, a general pipeline that combines visual observations with natural-language tasks or queries to construct persistent, grounded semantic representations. Arcadia transforms long-horizon visual data into semantic observations, object masks, spatial relevance heatmaps, graph-based memory, and query-driven inspection artifacts.

By organizing observations across time, viewpoints, and platforms into a shared semantic memory, Arcadia supports global scene understanding beyond individual frames and enables efficient inspection, retrieval, and downstream decision-making.

Approach

Arcadia transforms RGB imagery into persistent, task-aware spatial intelligence. A vision-language model identifies and prioritizes relevant observations, and video segmentation grounds them in the scene. The resulting output updates a 2D priority map that is overlaid on the reconstructed 3D environment, while a knowledge graph captures detected objects and their relationships. RGB imagery and GPS data are fused to build the 3D reconstruction.

Real-Time Processing

Arcadia processes incoming RGB video data and grounds a task such as “Find cars” in the observed scene.
